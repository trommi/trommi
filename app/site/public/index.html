From your phone

Your agents keep working. You keep the say.

    Chat with it, send it notes and pictures, follow the conversation.

  • See who is working

The loop

A question comes in. An answer goes back.

An agent that needs a decision should not guess, and should not wait in a terminal you are not looking at.

  1. An agent asks

    A Claude Code session files a card: the question, why it matters now, and a few options, with pictures where looks are in question.

  2. The card lies on your Desk

    One row per open question, the blocking ones first. A push tells your phone that something waits, without the content.

  3. You answer

    One tap on an option. Or ask back, hand the card back for rework, or put it off.

  4. The agent carries on

    The answer reaches the session that asked, and it goes on with the work.

On the Desk

A card holds one decision.

A card says what the question is and offers the options the agent stands behind. The one it would pick is marked, the way you would mark it with a highlighter.

  • Answers are tiles. One tap decides. A plain yes or no is answered straight from the list.
  • What?? asks the agent to explain the card in plain words.
  • Urgency comes from the agent. What blocks its work knocks; the rest waits.
  • The Scribble Board is the back of the Desk: draw something, select it, send it to a session.

Encryption

The server is a mailbox. It cannot open the mail.

Everything you and your agents write is encrypted on the device that writes it. To deliver the sealed envelopes, the server has to see some things. Here is which.

Sealed

Encrypted before it leaves a device.

  • Messages and drawings
  • Cards: titles, texts, options, your answers
  • The names of sessions, desks and devices
  • Files, with their names and types
  • The keys (the server holds none that opens anything)

Visible to the server

What it needs to route, order and ring.

  • Your email address
  • Your devices, a person's or an agent's, their public keys, and which of them share a session
  • Per envelope: sender, recipient, time, kind and size, and the card or chat it belongs to
  • Per card: open or answered, and its urgency
  • The number and size of files, and your push address
  • Whether an agent is connected and working

Your password never leaves your device. The server keeps a hash of a key derived from it, enough to check a login and not enough to open anything. Of a passkey it keeps the public key.

Nobody can recover your data for you. Lose every device and every way in (password, passkeys, Emergency Kit), and the content is gone. The other side of it: whoever holds one of your devices or a way in can read the whole history.

Every device has its own keys, each agent too. An agent joins only after you compared six emoji on both sides; a device of your own joins the same way, or by signing in to your account. Helpers that an agent starts are let in by that agent. A removed device can open nothing sent afterwards.

In a browser, the app's code comes from our server each time you open it. Whoever serves that code could read what the app reads: the encryption protects against a server that stores and forwards, not against one that sends other code.

Not audited yet. An independent audit of the cryptography is still to come.

Start

From nothing to the first question.

  1. Create an account

    At app.trommi.com, with an email address and a password or a passkey.

  2. Invite an agent

    Choose Invite Agent… in the app and run the command it shows in your project, on a machine with Claude Code:

    curl -fsSL https://app.trommi.com/connect | sh -s 'the invite link from the app'

  3. Compare six emoji

    The terminal and the app show the same six. Confirm, and that session's next question lands on your Desk.
